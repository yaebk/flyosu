What you are watching

The fly's view of the game is rendered onto its 8,452 photoreceptors. Notes become visible to it only in the lower half of the playfield, 250 ms before they should be hit, which is its scroll speed. The dimmed upper half shows upcoming notes for you, not for the fly.

The network runs in 2 ms steps with its synaptic weights fixed. The readout turns the activity of the simulated neurons into four drive signals, one per key. A key is pressed when its drive rises through zero, held while it stays up, and released when it falls. Timing is judged with osu!mania-style windows (MAX, 300, 200, 100, 50, MISS), and hold notes are judged on both head and release.

This is a simulation of the fly's wiring diagram, not a living fly, and the readout was trained. It shows what a fixed connectome plus a small fitted readout can play; it says nothing on its own about whether the real wiring beats a randomly rewired one.
